\section{Problem}
A campus with rooftop solar and a battery must decide every hour whether to store energy, use it, or buy from the grid. Prices change through the day, solar depends on weather, and outages strike unpredictably, so the value of stored energy depends on future events. This is a sequential decision problem under uncertainty with delayed consequences: a natural fit for RL, and one where fixed rules cannot anticipate outages.

\section{RL Formulation}
\subsection{Environment and data}
One episode is one day ($T{=}24$ hourly steps). Hourly all-sky irradiance $G_t$ and 2\,m temperature come from NASA POWER \cite{power} for one campus location, 2018--2023 (train 2018--21, validation 2022, held-out test 2023). PV output is $0.32\,G_t$ kW; non-critical load rises with temperature (cooling) and follows a daily shape; critical load is 60 kW; the time-of-day tariff is $\pi_t\in\{5,7,10\}$ Rs/kWh. The battery has 400 kWh capacity, $\pm$100 kW power and 95\% one-way efficiency. With probability 0.3 per day an outage of 2--4 h starts at a random hour in 08--20: the grid is unavailable, non-critical load is cut first, and any remaining shortfall is unserved critical load. Forecasts (next 3 h of solar) carry 10\% noise, and an outage warning fires with probability 0.7 shortly before an outage (0.1 false alarms). All physical constants, the tariff and the outage model are our assumptions; outages are simulated.

\subsection{State space $\mathcal S\subset\mathbb R^{11}$}
$s_t=[\,b_t,\ \sin\tfrac{2\pi h}{24},\ \cos\tfrac{2\pi h}{24},\ p_t,\ \hat p_{t+1},\hat p_{t+2},\hat p_{t+3},\ \pi_t,\ \ell_t,\ o_t,\ w_t\,]$: battery fraction, hour of day (2 features), current solar, noisy 3-hour solar forecast, price, total load, outage-active flag, and noisy outage warning, all scaled to roughly $[0,1]$.

\subsection{Action space $\mathcal A$ (discrete, $|\mathcal A|{=}6$)}
Battery mode $\in\{$charge, discharge, idle$\}$ $\times$ $\{$keep, shed non-critical load$\}$.

\subsection{Reward}
With $g_t$ the energy bought, $P_t$ the battery power, $u^{nc}_t$ the forced non-critical cut and $u^{c}_t$ the unserved critical energy:
\begin{equation}
r_t=-\tfrac{1}{1000}\big[\pi_t g_t + c_s\sigma_t \ell^{nc}_t + c_f u^{nc}_t + c_w|P_t| + \lambda c_c u^{c}_t\big],
\end{equation}
with $c_s{=}15$, $c_f{=}40$, $c_w{=}0.05$, $c_c{=}200$ (Rs/kWh) and $\sigma_t\in\{0,1\}$ the shed flag. Planned shedding is cheaper than a forced cut, so the agent can learn to shed before an outage. The constraint $\mathbb E[\sum_t u^c_t]\le d$ is handled by a Lagrangian relaxation \cite{cmdp}: every 20 episodes $\lambda\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(\lambda+\eta(\bar U-d),0,\lambda_{\max})$, where $\bar U$ is the recent mean daily unserved critical energy. Rewards are rebuilt from stored components with the current $\lambda$ at learning time.

\section{Algorithm and Baselines}
\textbf{Agent.} DQN \cite{dqn} with an MLP 11--128--128--6, replay buffer of 50k transitions, a target network synced every 500 updates, Huber loss, Adam (lr $10^{-3}$), $\gamma{=}0.99$, batch 64, and $\epsilon$-greedy decaying from 1 to 0.05 over the first 60\% of 3000 episodes. We add Double DQN \cite{ddqn} (online network selects, target network scores), a dueling head $Q=V+A-\overline{A}$ \cite{duel}, and prioritized replay \cite{per} ($\alpha{=}0.6$, importance weights with $\beta$ annealed 0.4$\to$1). Everything is implemented from scratch in PyTorch; no RL library or external repository is used.\\
\textbf{Baselines.} \emph{Idle} (never uses the battery); \emph{rule} (sell at peak price, store spare sun and cheap power, keep a 30\% reserve, shed during blackouts); \emph{oracle}, a dynamic program over 41 battery levels with perfect knowledge of that day's solar, load and outage. The oracle is an upper-bound benchmark, not our agent.\\
\textbf{Evaluation.} 200 fixed (day, outage) scenarios from the 2023 test year, identical for all policies, 3 training seeds. We report return, cost, unserved critical energy, and the gap closed $G=(R_{\text{agent}}-R_{\text{rule}})/(R_{\text{oracle}}-R_{\text{rule}})$. Return and $G$ are computed with $\lambda{=}1$ for every policy (constrained agents were trained with larger $\lambda$, so they are measured on a common yardstick rather than their own objective); ``cost'' is energy purchases plus shedding, forced-cut and wear penalties, and excludes the critical-load penalty, which is reported separately as unserved energy. \textbf{Protocol:} test year 2023, 200 fixed scenarios (seed 11), 3 training seeds (0--2); unconstrained variants use final checkpoints; constrained models use both final and best-validation checkpoints (selected on 2022); $\lambda{=}1$ for all policies.

All unconstrained variants beat the rule on cost and return ($G=71$--$81\%$), cutting cost by 15--17\% (the full agent: $G=79.7\pm2.9\%$, $-17.0\%$ cost). However, they leave more critical load unserved than the rule (0.44--0.82 vs.\ 0.30 kWh/day), so part of the saving comes from accepting more shortfall risk. DQN, Double, Dueling and the full agent differ by less than the seed-to-seed spread, so with three seeds we cannot rank them. Prioritized replay alone scored about 7--10 points lower than the other variants; a possible cause is that rare, high-error outage transitions dominate sampling and add noise, but we did not test this.

\textbf{Blackout constraint.} In both constrained configurations the multiplier $\lambda$ rose to its cap of 20 (a penalty of Rs\,4000/kWh on unserved critical load) and stayed at or near it for most of training: within about 500 episodes for the first run ($d{=}0.3$, $\eta{=}0.1$) and about 750 for v2 ($d{=}0.5$, $\eta{=}0.05$). The dual update therefore never reached equilibrium, and both runs behave like agents with a very large fixed penalty rather than enforcing their budgets as designed. The first run was unstable (validation return as low as $-19.8$): final checkpoints gave $G=59.0\pm12.9\%$ with 1.67 kWh unserved, and validation-selected checkpoints $65.5\pm7.6\%$ with $0.48\pm0.13$ kWh. Constrained v2 was steadier: $0.50\pm0.14$ kWh unserved, $G=69.4\pm4.4\%$, cost Rs\,12600. Neither matched unconstrained Double DQN (0.44 kWh, $G=80.6\%$). Since $\lambda$ saturated in both runs, we cannot say whether v2's improvement comes from the budget, the slower multiplier ramp, or seed noise. We suspect saturation arises because $\bar U$ is measured on $\epsilon$-greedy training episodes, which exceed the budget even late in training; we did not verify this.

\section{Limitations}
Outages, loads, tariff and battery/PV sizes are assumptions rather than measurements; a single site is used; NASA POWER values are satellite-derived on a coarse grid, not panel measurements; only three seeds were run, so small differences are not significant; the first constrained run (budget 0.3) did not meet its budget, and v2 met its relaxed budget of 0.5 only on average. Code, trained agents and reproduction commands are in the repository README.
